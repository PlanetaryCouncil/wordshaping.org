# Wordshaping

A lexicon of words that should already exist.

---

## disclaimerciously

*adverb* · dis-clai-MER-cious-ly

**1.** *(of an act)* In the manner of one who publishes something extravagant
while affixing a disclaimer that absolves nothing and advertises everything;
hedging performed as display.

**2.** *(of an object, via* disclaimercious*)* Bearing a disclaimer as
composition rather than as appendix — the hedge given margin, weight, and
iconography, integrated into the work it purports to qualify.

**Characteristics of disclaimerciousness.**

- *The borrowed triangle.* Regulatory gravitas with no regulator behind it;
  the warning glyph is typically the most austere element in an otherwise
  unrestrained frame.
- *A boast in the grammar of a warning.* The declared hazard is the
  achievement being advertised.
- *Escalation past the point of liability.* The scope widens until it covers
  something no disclaimer could cover, and the excess is the joke.
- *The asserted institution.* An authority is cited that exists solely because
  it was named — the disclaimer convenes the council that certifies it.

**Etymology.** `disclaimer` + `-ciously`, on the model of *capriciously*,
*speciously*, *graciously*. Retains the base noun whole. Stress shifts one
syllable rightward from *disclaimer* (cf. PHOtograph → phoTOGraphy).

**Note.** The buried *mercy* is not decorative. A disclaimer is a plea for
clemency entered in advance, and the word admits it under its breath.

**Forms.** disclaimercious *(adj.)* · disclaimerciousness *(n.)*

**Citation.**
> He posted this image disclaimerciously.

**Rejected variants.** *disclaimerouciously* (dead syllable at the `-erou-`
seam; two competing stress peaks) · *disclaimatiously* (mangles the base word
to reach its ending) · *disclaimsily* (different sense: the hedge that fools
nobody)

## followance

*noun* · FOL-low-ance

Subscription sustained without attention; the dutiful or ritual maintenance of
a following one does not read.

**Etymology.** `follow` + `-ance`, on the model of *attendance* (presence
without engagement) and *observance* (practice without belief). Phonologically
licensed by *allowance* — the identical `-ow` + `-ance` junction. No stress
shift; the stem keeps its accent.

**Distinguish.** *following* names the act, or the body of followers.
*followership* names the role. Neither names the condition — the several
thousand accounts subscribed to and never once seen. That is followance.

**Citation.**
> Her feed was mostly followance.

## followification

*noun* · fol-low-i-fi-CA-tion

The restructuring of a domain around the follow relation; the conversion of
colleagues, clergy, physicians, friends, and strangers alike into accounts one
subscribes to asymmetrically.

**Etymology.** `follow` + `-ification`, on the model of *gamification*,
*commodification*, *gentrification*.

**Register.** `-ify` resists plain Germanic stems, and where it takes them the
result is comic — *speechify*, *citify*, *argufy*. Followification therefore
lands as satire whether or not satire is intended. Usable, but not sayable
straight-faced.

**Forms.** followify *(v.)* · followified *(adj.)*

**Relation.** Followification is the process; [followance](#followance) is what
it leaves behind.

**Citation.**
> The followification of the newsroom.

## loopholification

*noun* · loop-hol-i-fi-CA-tion

The restructuring of a rule system around its exceptions, continued until the
exceptions constitute the system.

**Etymology.** `loophole` + `-ification`, on the model of *gentrification*.
Note the linking vowel: the suffix is `-ification` (from `-ify` + `-ation`),
never `-efication`. Cf. *classification*, *specification*, *purification*.

**Forms.** loopholify *(v.)* · loopholous *(adj.)* · loopholously *(adv.,* on
*frivolously, scandalously)*

**Relation.** Sits on the collection's spine: compliance with the letter of a
relation one is not actually in.

**Citation.**
> Procurement reform ended in quiet loopholification.

## intertwinification

*noun* · in-ter-twin-i-fi-CA-tion

Entanglement pursued as policy — the deliberate binding together of things
that were separate, so that they cannot later be separated.

**Etymology.** `intertwine` + `-ification`.

**Caveat.** `-ify` exists to derive verbs from non-verbs (*simple→simplify*,
*glory→glorify*). *Intertwine* is already a verb, so *intertwinify* is a round
trip, and the plain nominalization slot is held by **intertwinement**. The
suffix earns its place only under the systemic reading above — as
*gamification* means more than "making a game." For the mere state or act, use
*intertwinement*.

**Citation.**
> The contract was less a partnership than an intertwinification.

## ponderatiously

*adverb* · pon-der-A-tious-ly

In a manner that performs thought without conducting any; deliberation as
visible gesture — the held pause, the stroked chin, the *hmm* that precedes no
consideration.

**Etymology.** `ponder` + `-atious`, on the model of *ostentatious*,
*disputatious*, *vexatious*. `-atious` is the one adjective family in this
region still accepting new members.

**Blocked routes.** *ponderous* / *ponderously* already hold the `-ous` slot on
this root, in the unrelated sense of *heavy* (from *pondus*). *ponderance* is
shadowed by *preponderance*. And `-cious` was never available — see Formation
notes.

**Forms.** ponderatious *(adj.)* · ponderatiousness *(n.)*

**Citation.**
> He received the objection ponderatiously and answered it unchanged.

## infographicity

*noun* · in-fo-graph-IC-i-ty

The quality of reading as data while containing none; evidentiary appearance in
excess of evidentiary content. The bar chart without an axis; the arrow diagram
asserting a causation nobody measured.

**Etymology.** `infographic` + `-icity`, on the model of *specificity*,
*authenticity*, *historicity*. The `-ic` → `-icity` route is fully productive.

**Blocked route.** *infographicous* stacks `-ous` on `-ic`, which is already an
adjective suffix — cf. the absent *historicous*, *dramaticous*. The regular
adverb **infographically** exists by rule and needs no coining; it is neutral,
and carries none of the sense above.

**Prior art.** Tufte's *chartjunk* and *data-ink ratio* both concern ornament
burdening a graphic that is genuinely conveying data. Infographicity is the
rhetorical case: the visual grammar of information wrapped around none.

**Provenance.** Proposed as *infographicously* — an adverb, sense unstated.
The suffix was blocked on rule, but the replacement changed suffix, part of
speech, and meaning, the last of which was supplied rather than elicited. This
is the only entry in the collection not descended from the word that prompted
it. Retained pending a re-cut on the original terms.

**Citation.**
> The deck scored high on infographicity and reported no findings.

## reprogramification

*noun* · re-pro-gram-i-fi-CA-tion

The treatment of a mind, institution, or culture as a codebase awaiting a
patch; the doctrine that any settled thing can be rewritten on demand.

**Etymology.** `reprogram` + `-ification`.

**Caveat.** The same shape as [intertwinification](#intertwinification):
*reprogram* is already a verb, and **reprogramming** holds the plain
nominalization. For an ordinary mind ordinarily being rewired, use
*reprogramming* — shorter, and understood by everyone. The suffix earns its
keep only under the doctrinal reading above.

**Blocked route.** *reprogramificationously* — stacking; see Formation notes.
The deeper error is upstream: the intended sense is a **state**, not a manner,
and states take prefixes (*mid-reprogramming*), never `-ly`.

**Citation.**
> Every self-help shelf in the airport preaches reprogramification.

## unhappening

*noun* · un-HAP-pen-ing

The removal of an occurred event from the record; an incident officially
declared not to have taken place while the people who lived it remain.

**Pair.** *the unhappened* — harm that never occurred because someone prevented
it, the achievement that leaves no evidence. The inflection carries the whole
distinction: `-ed` never was, `-ing` was and then was not.

> Her entire career was unhappened.
> The incident was resolved by unhappening.

**Etymology.** `un-` + `happen`, plain reversal, as *undo*, *untie*, *unsay*.

**Blocked route.** *unhappenification* — `unhappen` is already a verb, so `-ify`
is a round trip; and *unhappening* is grasped on first contact, which
[the test](#formation-notes) prefers.

**Prior art.** Orwell's *memory hole* and *unpersoning* cover destroyed records
and erased people; *retcon* covers revised narrative. Unhappening is the
administrative case — the record amended, the bodies unamended.

## successitiously

*adverb* · suc-cess-I-tious-ly

In a manner that performs success; the win assembled after the fact, the metric
chosen once the number was known.

**Etymology.** `success` + `-itious`, on *fictitious*, *repetitious*,
*surreptitious*, *expeditious*. The nearest neighbour by sound is also the
nearest by sense.

**Category.** An **ear-word**, not a rule-word. `-itious` is inherited and no
longer productive; this works by echo, as [disclaimerciously](#disclaimerciously)
does. Honest to say so rather than dress music as morphology.

**Forms.** successitious *(adj.)* · successitiousness *(n.)*

**Note.** *successification* is legitimately formed — *success* is a noun,
`-ify` takes nouns, no squatter holds the slot, and the chain runs exactly like
*class → classify → classification*. It is omitted only for shelf balance; see
the appendix question in Formation notes.

**Citation.**
> The quarter was reported successitiously.

## Formation notes

**One suffix, one move.** Each derivational suffix names a destination:
`-ification` → systemic process, noun. `-ously` → manner, adverb. They are
alternate roads, not successive rungs, and cannot be stacked — the only legal
exit from `-ification` is `-ationally`, which is flat bureaucratic English and
costs the root whatever charm it had. Chains like *X-ificatiously* gain
syllables without gaining density, which is the characteristic failure of
manufactured words.

**Adverbs come off the root, not off the noun.** `disclaimerciously` works
because it makes a single move from `disclaimer` to manner. Derive
`loopholously` from *loophole*, not from *loopholification*.

**Found beats built.** A coinage should read as recovered rather than
invented. Suffix stacking is the clearest tell of the latter.

**`-cious` is inherited, not productive.** Every genuine member descends from a
stem in `-ce/-ice`: *malice→malicious*, *caprice→capricious*,
*avarice→avaricious*, *grace→gracious*, *space→spacious*. There is no live rule
to invoke. [disclaimerciously](#disclaimerciously) is not a counterexample —
*disclaimer* ends in /mər/, which happens to glide into /ʃəs/, and the accident
delivered *mercy* as a bonus. That was ear, not rule, and it does not port to
other roots. The live neighbouring family is `-atious` (*ostentatious*,
*flirtatious*, *vexatious*), which still takes new members.

**Derivational suffixes don't attach past an `-ing`** — `-ous`, `-ity`, `-ify`
all need the root: *ponder*, not *pondering*. But `-ly` does, productively:
*lovingly*, *knowingly*, *jokingly*, *strikingly*, *willingly*. So
**ponderingly** was legal from the start, formed off *pondering* exactly as
first proposed, and [ponderatiously](#ponderatiously) was never needed to reach
that sense. An earlier version of this note overstated the rule and sent a good
form to the scrap heap.

**Check for squatters before building.** A root whose `-ous` or `-ance` slot is
already occupied in an unrelated sense (*ponderous* from *pondus*,
*preponderance*) forces you to another family or another root. Collision is
usually discovered after the word is loved, which is too late.

**One suffix per collection is one too many.** If every entry ends the same
way, the lexicon stops being a set of words and becomes a joke about a suffix.

**Check whether regular morphology already fills the slot.** *ponderously* and
*infographically* both exist by rule and killed a proposed coinage each. Before
building, derive the form the rules would already produce. Say it aloud: if it
sounds merely unfamiliar rather than invented, it is not a coinage — it is
English you hadn't happened to use.

**Not every meaning wants an adverb.** A manner adverb answers *how was it
done*. A state answers *what condition was it done from*, and English marks
states with prefixes and prepositions — *mid-sentence*, *mid-reprogramming*,
*under protest* — never with `-ly`. Forcing a state through the adverb slot is
what produces six-syllable monsters; the suffix is the wrong door, not the
wrong size.

**Inspect the typos.** The strongest word here arrived by accident — *mercy*
was never designed into [disclaimerciously](#disclaimerciously), it was found
sitting in it. A slipped letter is worth reading aloud before it is corrected.

**`-ic` takes `-ally`, not `-ly`.** *basically, historically, dramatically,
tragically*. The sole exception in English is **publicly**.

**When a proposal is blocked, ask what it was for.** Ruling out a suffix is not
permission to substitute a different word wearing the same root. See the
provenance note under [infographicity](#infographicity) — the rule was right and
the replacement was still an imposition.

**"That I understand" is the test.** A regular form like *infographically* is
grasped on first contact by someone who has never met it, because the rules
assembled it rather than a person. No coinage in this file can do that; each one
costs a paragraph of explanation. That price is worth paying only where no
regular form fills the slot — and it usually does.
