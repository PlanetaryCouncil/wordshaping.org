Eight false seams

Chunks heard as single suffixes where the join actually falls elsewhere. Three of the eight are now in dictionaries or academic use. Nothing distinguishes them from the other five except that enough people repeated them.

How the words were made

One suffix, one moveEach suffix names a destination. They are alternate roads, not successive rungs — -ification plus -ously gains syllables without gaining density. The legal exit from -ification is -ationally.

-ification wants a nounLoophole, success, compost, remix, hack, war worked. Intertwine, reprogram, unhappen, prevent, expand are round trips, because -ify's only job is making a verb out of something that is not one.

Stress travels one syllable, never twodis·CLAIM·er becomes dis·clai·MER·cious·ly and holds. BRAIN·fart and le·GIT·i·mate cannot reach. Monosyllabic bases can never fail this test.

Compounds buy syllablesSecondary stress keeps a long tail from collapsing. WORD·smith·ing·ly survives four syllables; VIC·to·ri·ful cannot.

Clip the base to fix the meterLong word, early stress, suffix that pulls right? Shorten the base and the stress comes to meet the suffix instead of failing to reach it. legitimate → legit.

Libfixes want short, neutral bases outside their home fieldOne or two syllables; the suffix supplies the judgment, so an already-evaluative base is redundant; and the field the fragment came from is saturated.

The suffix must carry the meaningNot just the part of speech. -licious encodes appetite, -ification encodes process. Neither encodes inversion — that is prefix territory.

-ness and -ish are terminal for suffixationNothing derives further from them. Inflection, conversion and compounding still work.

Check whether the rules already built itSay it aloud. If it sounds merely unfamiliar rather than invented, it is not a coinage — it is English you had not happened to use. Ponderously, infographically, unhingedly, ponderingly, litigious and victoriously each killed a candidate.

Inspect the typosThree accidents paid: mercy inside the founding word, Middle English -tioun from a slipped u, and a three-hundred-year-old split between flour and flower.

Uptake decides, not rulesThese rules describe what English currently does. They are weather reports, not permits. Three of eight false seams here are in dictionaries, and nothing distinguishes them from the other five except that enough people repeated them.
