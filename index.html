invationyours
"invitation to invasion — a new category, merging in"		 New category: blending. Everything prior was affixation or compounding; this is a portmanteau, where the parents share material rather than sit adjacent. First one made deliberately — floordrobe was found, not built. Unusually well-seated. Both parents share two seams — the in- prefix and the -ion ending — where most blends overlap at one (br+unch, sm+og, mot+otel). And they are semantically adjacent: both carry Latin in-, "into." Invitation is calling someone in; invasion is going in uninvited. Same act, opposite permission, and the word sits on the hinge. Referent is real: entry legitimised by an invitation the enterer arranged. "Invited intervention" recurs in international law disputes; so do terms of service, consent banners where accepting is the entry, and the guest who treats one invitation as standing access. The legiticiously problem in physical form.

Weakness: invasion is loud, invitation survives only as that lone t. invitasion keeps both audible at one syllable more — economy against legibility.		 175·4·4·4·—

Libfixes — the seam problem, vindicated. A mis-heard join, extracted and reused, is how English manufactures suffixes. -gate (Watergate is a hotel), -athon (Marathon is a place), -holic (alcohol+ic), -tastic (fantastic isn't fan+tastic), -splain (ex+plain), -burger (Hamburg+er), -licious (Latin deliciosus). Arnold Zwicky's term for these is libfix. The ear that hears -fication as one solid chunk is running the same mechanism that produced hackathon and mansplain. The only difference between the error and the innovation is uptake.
Correction. An earlier note here claimed no suffix attaches past an -ing. Wrong, and it cost you a word. Only derivational suffixes are blocked (-ous, -ity, -ify); -ly attaches freely and productively — lovingly, knowingly, jokingly, strikingly. ponderingly was legal from the start, formed off pondering exactly as first written. Ponderatiously was never needed to reach that sense.
The seam problem. Five chunks heard as single suffixes. Four are really joins, with the first piece belonging to the base: -fication is -ify+-ation (the i was dropped); -ifully is a -y noun + -ful+-ly (an i was added); -cious needs a -ce stem; -alistic is a -al base + -istic (natural, ideal, material). The fifth, -licious, is a genuine productive libfix — the identical move, made right by uptake. These are all real rhythmic units, which is why the coinages scan. The ear is correct about the music and blind to the seam, and that is precisely the faculty that manufactures new suffixes. Four wrong, one born.
The -ification diagnostic. Nine proposals sort with no exceptions. Noun bases work — loophole, success, follow (n.). Verb bases are round trips — intertwine, reprogram, unhappen, prevent, expand — because -ify's only job is making a verb out of something that isn't one. Give it a verb and it has nothing to do, so the plain -ing or -ion form wins. The suffix is your signature move and two of the strongest words here came from it; it just needs a noun.

The rubric

Five axes, 0–5 each, 25 total. Scores are shown as slot·form·clarity·music·spine. Provisional and arguable — rewrite the axes if they measure the wrong things.

Slot

Is the meaning unoccupied?

Does an existing word already cover it? Ponderously and infographically each killed a candidate by already being there.

Form

Is the derivation legal?

Right suffix, right base, right linking vowel, no stacking. An honest ear-word scores well here; a disguised one does not.

Clarity

Understood on first contact?

Your test. Would a stranger guess it without a paragraph of explanation?

Music

Does it sound like a word?

Stress, syllable count, dead weight — plus any buried gift, like the mercy in disclaimerciously.

Spine

Does it fit the collection?

So far every strong entry names one thing: the performance of a relation nobody is actually in.
